# Writing the Whiteboard — tips for clean transcription

For when Claude reads a whiteboard photo and enters the scores (see the
whiteboard score-entry protocol). These habits at the board make the numbers
unambiguous and cut transcription errors to near zero. Companion to
`Athletes booking list.md` (the whiteboard name → athlete map).

1. **Make 0 and 5 unmistakable.** Write **0 as a full closed loop**, and **5 with
   a clear flat top + a distinct round belly**. This is the #1 confusable pair
   (an S386 misread had 40 read as 45).

2. **Decimals = a dot, written clearly.** Chris uses **`37.5`** (dot, not a comma).
   Make the dot and the 5 crisp; no trailing dots ("40." is ambiguous).

3. **Cross your 7s, foot your 1s.** A barred 7 and a 1 with a base keep the
   second-most-confusable pair apart.

4. **Keep columns aligned.** Write each athlete's numbers directly under the
   column headers (e.g. OHP×3 / OHP×1 / PP×3 / PP×1), evenly spaced — stops a
   value drifting into the wrong column.

5. **Fresh, fine marker.** A dying or fat tip blurs the tail of a 5 or 0 into the
   other; a crisp tip preserves the detail.

6. **Photo: straight-on, good light, no glare, fill the frame.** A sharp, square
   photo lets the per-cell zoom do its job.
